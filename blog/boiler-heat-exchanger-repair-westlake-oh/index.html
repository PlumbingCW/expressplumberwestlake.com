Boiler Heat Exchanger Repair in Westlake, OH

The heat exchanger passes combustion heat into the boiler water. A cracked one can leak combustion gases or water and usually means replacement. Scale and sediment problems can often be cleaned, so a proper diagnosis comes first.

By Westlake Express Plumbing · Published November 2026

In a hot water boiler, a burner heats a metal heat exchanger, and the exchanger gives that heat to the water that circulates to radiators or baseboards. Because the exchanger sits between flames and water, it takes the most thermal stress of any part in the boiler.

It also hides the signs of trouble. A failing exchanger can look like a lot of other faults, so the first step is a careful diagnosis, not a guess. If someone tells you the heat exchanger is bad, ask to see the evidence.

Signs of heat exchanger trouble

SignWhat it may meanNext step
Kettling or banging noisesScale or sediment on the exchanger surfacesDescale or flush the boiler
Repeated pressure lossA leak in the system, possibly the exchangerPressure test and inspect
Water pooling under the boilerA leaking joint, valve or exchangerTurn off and call
Soot or yellow flamePoor combustion or a blocked exchangerCall right away
Carbon monoxide alarmCombustion gases are entering the houseLeave the house and call the gas utility or 911

Carbon monoxide is the signal to take seriously. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the building, get fresh air and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not try to troubleshoot the boiler first.

Can a heat exchanger be repaired?

It depends on the problem. Scale and sediment can often be removed with a chemical or mechanical clean, and that restores heat transfer and ends the noise. A small leak at a joint or a connection may be repaired by replacing a gasket or a fitting.

A crack in the exchanger itself is another matter. A cracked exchanger cannot be safely patched in most boilers, and the manufacturer's manual usually says to replace the part or the boiler. A fix that cuts corners is a hazard.

Repair or replace: how we think about it

Repair when

The exchanger is sound, the problem is scale or a gasket and the boiler is otherwise in good shape.

Replace the part when

The model is current, the part is available and the rest of the boiler has life left.

Replace the boiler when

The exchanger is cracked in an old unit, parts are scarce or the efficiency is far below current models.

A new boiler is a significant decision, and we give you a clear price before work starts. Our boiler repair page lists what we check on a service call, and water heater installation applies if your hot water comes from a separate tank.

Westlake notes: winter, thermostats and make-up water

Cleveland Water's winter tips recommend keeping the thermostat at 55 degrees Fahrenheit or higher if you are away, and opening cabinet doors so heat reaches pipes under sinks during extreme cold. A boiler that fails in a hard freeze puts the pipes at risk, so act quickly if the heat goes out.

A boiler is a closed system and adds little fresh water. If yours needs frequent make-up water, that is a sign of a leak, not a normal habit. Cleveland Water's 2025 report lists moderately hard water, with hardness averaging 117 as calcium carbonate, and every gallon of fresh water brings minerals that can scale the exchanger. Fixing the leak solves both problems.

A fall tune-up checklist

  • Check system pressure against the manufacturer's range.
  • Inspect the burner, flame color and venting.
  • Test the pressure relief valve and look at the expansion tank.
  • Check circulator pumps and zone valves.
  • Look for corrosion, weeping joints and water stains.
  • Bleed radiators and clear baseboards of dust and furniture.

When the boiler is also your hot water

Some boilers heat domestic hot water through a coil or an indirect tank. If showers run cold while radiators are warm, the issue may be in that part. See water heater repair for related faults.

How a proper diagnosis goes

A technician should check the basics before blaming the exchanger. They read the system pressure, look at the expansion tank, run the burner and watch the flame, test for leaks and check the venting. A combustion analyzer measures what comes out of the flue, and that reading says a lot about whether the exchanger is clogged or cracked.

If a crack is suspected, the technician looks for evidence such as a visible split, water on the burner or a pressure test that fails when everything else holds. You are entitled to see it. A good technician will show you the photo or the part and explain why it is the cause.

Planning for the winter you want

Boiler trouble tends to show up on the coldest day. If your boiler is old, make a plan before it fails: know the age, know the model and decide what you would do if the heat went out. A quick fall check is cheaper than an emergency call in January, and it gives you time to compare repair and replacement without pressure.

Questions to ask the technician

  • What exactly failed, and can you show me?
  • Is the part available, and what is the warranty?
  • What does repair involve compared with replacing the boiler?
  • Is the venting and the combustion safe after the work?
  • How soon can the work be done, and how will the house stay warm until then?

A technician who answers these plainly is giving you the information you need. If a quote pushes you to decide without answers, take a moment and ask again.

What to do if the boiler fails or a CO alarm sounds

  1. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.
  2. If there is no heat, check the thermostat, the power switch and the pressure gauge.
  3. Do not relight a boiler that has shut itself off more than once.
  4. Protect the pipes in a freeze by keeping the house as warm as you can and opening cabinet doors.
  5. Call for service and describe the symptoms and any error codes.

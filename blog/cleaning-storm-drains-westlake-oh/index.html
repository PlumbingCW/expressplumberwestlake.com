Cleaning Out Storm Drains in Westlake, OH

Storm drains fall into two groups. Street inlets belong to the city, and yard and downspout drains are usually yours. Clear leaves from the surface, never reach into a pipe and call the city or a plumber for anything deeper.

By Westlake Express Plumbing · Published November 2026

A storm drain carries rainwater away from streets, yards and roofs. It is separate from the sanitary sewer that carries wastewater from toilets and sinks, though in some older areas the two systems are connected. When a storm drain backs up, water pools in the street or in the yard, and in the worst case it enters a basement.

The first question is who owns the drain. Street inlets and the pipes under the street belong to the city. Yard drains, downspout connections and the lines under your driveway usually belong to the homeowner. Knowing which one is blocked decides who to call.

Types of storm drains around a house

DrainWho usually owns itWhat to do
Street inlet or catch basinThe cityClear leaves from the grate from the surface, or report it to the city
Yard drain or catch basinHomeownerClear debris from the grate and flush with a hose
Downspout leader or underground lineHomeownerCheck the connection and flush, then call a plumber if it stays slow
Driveway or patio drainHomeownerClear the grate and test the flow
Foundation drain tileHomeownerTied to a sump pit, which a plumber can inspect

What you can safely do yourself

  1. Wait until the rain has eased and stay out of moving water.
  2. Wear gloves and use a rake or a gloved hand to remove leaves and debris from the surface of a grate.
  3. Run a garden hose into the drain to see if it flows.
  4. Check that downspouts are connected and the extensions run away from the house.
  5. Note where water backs up and take photos.

Do not reach into a pipe or a catch basin, and do not open a storm drain cover. Heavy lids are dangerous, and pipes can hold sharp debris. If a drain has a large object in it or stays full, stop and ask for help.

When to call a plumber

  • The yard drain stays full for hours after the rain stops.
  • A hose in the drain does not flow away.
  • Water comes up in the basement floor drain during a storm.
  • A downspout line has collapsed or filled with roots.
  • You suspect the storm line is connected to the sanitary sewer.

A plumber can clear a yard or downspout line with a cable machine or with hydro jetting, and check the pipe with a camera. Our drain cleaning page describes the method. If the issue shows up as a wet basement, see basement waterproofing and sump pump service.

Preventing storm drain trouble

A short seasonal routine

  • Autumn. Clear leaves from yard drains and gutters before they clog the line.
  • Late winter. Check that downspout extensions are not frozen or buried in ice.
  • Spring. Flush yard drains with a hose and test the sump pump.
  • After a storm. Look for pooling and note any slow drains.

Gutters, grading and the foundation

Most storm drain trouble around a house starts with the roof. Gutters that overflow dump water at the foundation, and downspouts that end beside the wall send it into the soil next to the basement. Fixing those is cheaper than any drain repair.

Grade the soil so it slopes away from the house, and extend downspouts well past the foundation. If water keeps pooling anyway, the yard drain or the line may be undersized or blocked, and it is time for a plumber.

A note on roots and old lines

Tree roots grow into storm lines through joints, just as they do in sewer laterals. An older clay or concrete line is a common target. A camera inspection shows roots and breaks, and a cut and a jet clear them. If the line is broken, repair is the only lasting fix.

A short checklist for a stormy week

  • Walk the yard before the rain and clear leaves from grates and gutters.
  • Make sure downspout extensions are attached and point away from the house.
  • Check that the sump pump runs and the discharge is clear.
  • Know where your floor drain cleanout is, in case you need access.
  • Move valuables off the basement floor if heavy rain is forecast.

Taking those steps takes less than an hour and covers the most common storm drain problems around a house. If water still backs up after a storm, take photos and note the time and the rainfall. That record helps the city and a plumber work out where the problem is.

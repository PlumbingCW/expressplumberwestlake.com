How to Replace a Water Heater Heating Element in Westlake, OH

An electric water heater has two elements, and either can fail. Replace one by cutting power at the breaker, draining the tank, unscrewing the old element, fitting a matching new one and refilling before the power goes back on.

By Westlake Express Plumbing · Published November 2026

If your electric water heater makes lukewarm water or runs out quickly, an element is the usual suspect. The upper element heats the top of the tank first, and the lower element does most of the work. When one burns out, the heater still works, but badly.

Replacing an element is a manageable job for someone comfortable with basic electrical safety. Water and electricity share a small space, so the steps below put safety first. If any step feels uncertain, stop and call a plumber.

Confirm the element has failed

SymptomLikely failureCheck
No hot water at allUpper element or thermostat, or tripped breakerCheck the breaker, then test the elements
Lukewarm waterLower element failedTest the lower element
Hot water runs out fastLower element failed or heavy sedimentTest the lower element, then flush
Breaker keeps trippingShorted element or wiring faultStop and call a professional

A multimeter set to measure resistance can test an element once the power is off and the wires are disconnected. A reading near zero or infinite resistance, rather than a reasonable value, means the element has failed. Check the manual for the expected number.

Safety first

  1. Turn off the breaker for the water heater at the panel.
  2. Confirm the power is off with a non-contact voltage tester at the heater.
  3. Close the cold water shutoff above the heater.
  4. Open a hot tap in the house to let air in.
  5. Attach a hose to the drain valve and drain the tank below the level of the element.

Never open the element access panel with the power on. Do not turn the heater on with an empty tank, because a dry element burns out in seconds.

Replacing the element

  1. Remove the access panel and the insulation.
  2. Disconnect the wires and take a photo of how they were connected.
  3. Use an element wrench to unscrew the old element counterclockwise.
  4. Check the new element matches the wattage, voltage and shape.
  5. Fit the new gasket, thread in the element by hand and tighten with the wrench.
  6. Reconnect the wires, replace the insulation and the panel.

Fill the tank completely before restoring power. Open the cold supply, keep a hot tap open until water runs steadily with no air, then check the element for leaks. Only then switch the breaker on.

Westlake notes: why elements burn out and who to ask

Scale is a common reason elements fail. Cleveland Water's 2025 report lists hardness, as calcium carbonate, with an average of 117 and a range of 105 to 130, which is moderately hard water. Scale coats the element, traps heat and shortens its life. If your old element was crusted with white or tan deposits, a flush of the tank is a good follow-up.

Permit rules for water heater work vary by city. Westlake's city website lists Building Permits and Registered Contractors but does not say whether an element swap needs one. Call City Hall at 440-871-3300 if you are unsure. A like-for-like part swap generally differs from replacing the heater, but the city is the right source.

Flush the tank while you are at it

Sediment settles at the bottom of the tank and covers the lower element. Since the tank is already drained, open the cold supply for a short time with the drain valve open to stir and flush it out. Do this until the water runs clear.

A tank that has been neglected for years may release a lot of sediment, and the drain valve can clog. If it does, do not force it. A clogged valve may call for a plumber with the right tools.

When to stop and call a plumber

  • The tank is leaking from the body or the base.
  • The element will not turn or the threads are damaged.
  • You see burned or melted wiring.
  • The breaker trips even after the element is replaced.
  • The heater is gas, not electric. A gas unit has no element, and its repairs differ.

Choosing the right replacement element

Elements are made in different wattages, voltages and shapes. The data plate on the heater lists the wattage and voltage, and the old element has the same numbers stamped on it. Match them. Too high a wattage overloads the wiring and the thermostat, and too low gives weak heating.

Screw-in elements are common, and bolt-in flange types appear on some models. Low-density elements run cooler on the surface, and they last longer in moderately hard water because they scale less. Ask the parts counter about them.

After the repair: what to expect

A full tank takes time to heat. Give it a few hours before judging the result. If the water is still lukewarm after that, the thermostat may be at fault, or the other element may have failed too. If it gets very hot, check that the thermostat is set properly, since the temperature and pressure relief valve is a last safety, not a control.

Tools and materials for the job

  • A non-contact voltage tester and a multimeter.
  • An element wrench sized for the element type.
  • A garden hose for draining the tank.
  • A new element and gasket that match the data plate.
  • A flashlight, gloves and eye protection.

Gather these before you start. The job moves fast once the tank is drained, and you do not want to leave the heater open while you run to the store.

Water heater work in Ohio falls under the Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). A like-for-like element swap is a repair, but if you disturb the relief valve or piping, the code applies to what you leave behind.

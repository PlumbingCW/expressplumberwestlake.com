How hot water heat works

A boiler heats water and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboard units. The water gives up its heat and returns to the boiler to be heated again. Unlike a forced-air furnace, a boiler heats through the surfaces in each room, so heat feels even and quiet.

Older houses in Northeast Ohio often have hot water boilers, some of them decades old. Many keep working with regular care, though the parts wear, and the efficiency of an old unit is low.

Heat exchanger worries

The heat exchanger is the part that transfers combustion heat to the water. Cracks or scale in it cause noise, poor heating and leaks. Repairing an exchanger is sometimes possible, but often the right answer is replacement of the unit. We can inspect yours and tell you which case it is.

What a seasonal tune-up looks at

Pre-winter boiler checks

  • Pressure and expansion tank. The gauge should sit in the range the manufacturer gives, and the tank should hold air.
  • Venting and combustion. A clear vent and a healthy flame matter for efficiency and safety.
  • Valves and circulator pumps. Stuck valves and weak pumps leave rooms cold.
  • Leaks and corrosion. Small weeps at joints get worse over a season.

If you smell gas or carbon monoxide alarms sound, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility. Do not stay and try to fix it yourself. Call us for plumbing questions only after you are safely outside.

Water heating and the boiler

Some boilers also heat domestic hot water. If your hot water comes from the boiler and your showers run cold, see water heater repair for the tank side, or ask us whether the boiler's indirect tank is the issue. A separate water heater installation may help in houses where the boiler is aging.

Keeping a boiler going through a Cleveland winter

Northeast Ohio winters are long, and a boiler works hardest when the weather is worst. A small fault in November becomes a no-heat call in January, which is why a fall check is worth the visit. Bleed radiators, clear furniture from around baseboard units and keep the area around the boiler free of storage.

If the house will be cold while the boiler is down, protect the pipes. Open cabinet doors, let a faucet drip on exterior walls and keep the thermostat as high as safely possible. A frozen pipe is a second problem you do not want. See burst pipe repair for what to do if one splits.

Boiler replacement or keep repairing

Replacement becomes the sensible choice when repairs are frequent, the heat exchanger has failed or the unit is far below modern efficiency. We describe the options and give you a clear price before work starts. A boiler that runs well with routine care does not need to be replaced just because it is old.

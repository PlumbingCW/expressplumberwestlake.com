Commercial plumbing is not residential plumbing at a larger scale. Pipes are larger, buildings share systems between tenants, hours are long and downtime costs money. We take commercial calls for offices, retail, restaurants and mixed-use buildings in Westlake and nearby towns.

After-hours calls from tenants and managers

A tenant in a shared building often cannot find the shutoff, and a manager may be across town. Our dispatchers ask where the water is coming from, which floor and what other tenants are below, then guide the caller to the right valve. That quick triage reduces the damage.

Tell us the building's address, your role and whether the building has a property manager's emergency number. We can then coordinate with the right people.

Grease traps and kitchen lines

Restaurants and food service run grease through the plumbing every day. A grease trap catches much of it, but only if it is emptied before it fills. A full trap lets grease reach the building drain, where it hardens and blocks the line.

We help with kitchen line cleaning and with hydro jetting when cable work leaves a film behind. Grease trap pumping is often a separate hauler's job, and we will tell you when it is time to call one.

Backflow and testing

Many commercial buildings have backflow prevention devices that protect the water supply from contamination. Testing is a requirement in many places and usually has a set schedule. Rules depend on the water utility and the building, so ask Cleveland Water or the Westlake Building Department what applies to your property.

Planning ahead

Regular maintenance cuts the number of emergency calls. A short visit to check the water heater, floor drains and key valves reveals trouble early. See plumbing installation for buildout work, and water heater replacement when a tank has failed.

Keep a list of shutoff locations for the building, including the main, the water heater and each restroom group. Share it with staff. In an emergency, finding the right valve in the first minute matters more than anything else.

Backflow protection and fixture counts in commercial buildings follow the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Tenants should ask the building owner what applies to their space.

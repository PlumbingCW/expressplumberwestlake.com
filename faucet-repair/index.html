Emergency Faucet Repair in Westlake, OH

A faucet that will not shut off can flood a sink in minutes. Call (440) 468-0237, 24/7. Close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise, and if there are none, shut the main water valve.

Most faucet problems are small parts wearing out. A cartridge loses its seal, a washer hardens, an O-ring cracks or a valve seat corrodes. The fix is usually a part, not a new faucet, though an old unit with worn internal parts may be better replaced.

A dripping faucet seems trivial, but Cleveland Water notes that a faucet dripping about once per second can waste over 3,000 gallons a year. That cost shows up on the water bill, and the drip can stain the sink.

When water will not stop

Faucet or supply line leaking out of control

Turn the angle stops under the sink clockwise to close them. If a stop is stuck or leaks when you turn it, shut the main valve and call right away. Place a bucket under the leak and move cleaning supplies and stored items out of the cabinet.

A stuck or failed shutoff valve under a sink is a repair we do often. It is worth replacing old stops at the same time as the faucet, because a valve that will not close turns every future repair into a main-valve job.

Repair or replace

If the faucet is relatively new and the finish is good, a cartridge swap is the sensible choice. If the faucet is corroded, the body is cracked or parts are no longer available, replacement is cheaper in the long run.

We install the faucet you choose and test the supply lines. If you are redoing a kitchen, we can also handle the disposal and sink connections in the same visit.

Low flow and mineral buildup

A faucet with weak flow often has a clogged aerator rather than a plumbing fault. Cleveland Water's 2025 report shows moderately hard water, with hardness averaging 117 as calcium carbonate, and mineral scale is a common cause of clogged aerators. Unscrew the aerator and soak it in vinegar. If flow is still weak at several taps, call us and see the leak detection page for hidden causes.

Replacing the supply lines at the same time

Braided supply lines under the sink are a common leak source because they age out of sight. If your faucet is being replaced, replacing both supply lines costs little extra and removes a failure point. Check the lines for rust on the nuts, bulges in the braid or damp spots on the cabinet floor.

Dry the area around the faucet base and check again in a few hours. A damp ring after a night means the base seal or a supply line is leaking even if the spout is not dripping.

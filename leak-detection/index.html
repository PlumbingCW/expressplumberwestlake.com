Start with the meter

Cleveland Water suggests a quick test before you call anyone. Turn off every tap and appliance in the house, find the water meter, and look at the test wheel on its face. If the wheel keeps turning with no water in use, there is a leak somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

That simple check tells you whether you have a leak, and it tells you something about size. A slowly creeping wheel suggests a drip, while a fast one points to a pipe that is open.

Common hidden leaks

Toilet tanks

Cleveland Water says a running toilet can waste 200 gallons or more a day. Add dye to the tank and wait 15 to 30 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a leaking flapper.

Supply lines under sinks

Slow drips from angle stops and braided lines can run for months behind cabinet doors.

Water heater connections

A small weep at a fitting dries before you see it, and corrodes the fitting from outside.

Service line

The pipe from the street to the meter can leak underground without any sign inside the house.

How we find a leak without opening walls

We start with the meter test and a pressure check on the supply side. Acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging and moisture meters then narrow down the location. The goal is to open the smallest possible area to reach the pipe.

If the leak is under a slab, we explain the options. A pinhole in a copper line may be repaired in place, while a pattern of leaks often points to repiping as the longer-term answer.

Active leak or water through the ceiling: emergency steps

Shut off the main water valve. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain remaining pressure, move belongings away from the wet area and turn off power to wet areas only from a dry spot.

A leak that was found late: the bill and the repair

Cleveland Water says customers who repair an undetected underground leak may receive up to 50 percent off excess consumption for one billing cycle by submitting a Plumbing Repair Statement. Ask the utility about the current process, and keep the plumber's paperwork. We document what we found and what we repaired.

Cleveland Water also notes that it repairs leaks between the street main and the curb stop. The customer is responsible for the service line from there, which includes the stretch from the tree lawn to the meter.

If the leak turns out to be a failed tank, water heater repair covers it. If it is under a fixture, our faucet repair and toilet repair pages explain the fixes.

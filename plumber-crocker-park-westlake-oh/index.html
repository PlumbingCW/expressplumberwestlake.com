Crocker Park sits beside the Promenade shopping center, close to where I-90, Crocker Road and Detroit Road meet. The City of Westlake calls it the largest development in the city's history. It opened in part in November 2004 and mixes shops, restaurants, offices and housing in one walkable district.

That mix makes a plumbing call here different from a call in a street of single-family houses. This page explains what we can and cannot say about plumbing at Crocker Park, who usually handles which problem, and when to call us.

What Crocker Park is, from the city's own sources

The city's history pages describe Main Street with first-floor shops and apartments or offices above, a median park with fountains in the center, and for-sale housing inside the western perimeter. About 70 percent of the original project was built or approved by 2008, and voters approved an expansion in November 2007.

We did not find a public count of homes or units, so we do not give one. We also could not confirm building ages beyond the 2004 opening of the first phase, or what pipe materials were used in any one building.

Plumbing needs that fit a mixed-use district

Where shops and homes share a district, the same few problems come up in every kind of building. A restaurant or food shop pushes grease through its drains, so hydro jetting and drain cleaning are common asks. An apartment with no hot water usually needs a look at the heater, and water heater repair explains what we check.

A hidden leak in a wall or ceiling is harder to trace in a building with units above and below. Leak detection covers how we find the source before anyone opens a wall.

Water coming through a ceiling or wall: do this first

If water is moving, shut off the nearest fixture valve. If you cannot stop it, tell the property manager at once and ask where the building shutoff is.

Keep away from wet outlets and light fixtures. Call (440) 468-0237 if you need a plumber and tell us the unit and floor.

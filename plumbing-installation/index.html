Planning a basement bathroom

Below-grade plumbing has one extra challenge, which is getting waste to the sewer. If the sewer line sits higher than the basement floor, a sewage ejector basin or a macerating system lifts waste up and out. If it sits lower, the drain can run by gravity, with rough-in work under the slab.

We check the existing drain depth and the location of the main stack before drawing a plan. Cutting the slab for new drain lines is a normal part of the job, and we explain the order of work so you know when concrete is poured and patched.

Permits and inspections

New plumbing work generally involves a permit and an inspection. Permit rules differ by city, so ask the Westlake Building Department which ones apply to your project. The Cuyahoga County Board of Health also publishes a plumbing plan review and permit application for new plumbing, which you may need depending on the work.

What a clear price looks like

A fixture install priced from a menu is simple. A new bathroom is not, because the cost depends on access, distance to the stack and what has to be opened. We walk the space, explain the steps and give you a clear price before work starts.

If the walls are already open for a remodel, tell us. We can often schedule our rough-in work around the other trades and save you a second round of drywall.

Retail, offices and mixed-use buildings

Westlake's commercial districts, such as the Crocker Park mixed-use area and the office buildings along Crocker Road, ask more of plumbing than a house does. Tenant buildouts add restrooms, break-room sinks and dishwashers on tight schedules, and landlords often need after-hours work so tenants are not disturbed.

If you are fitting out a space, tell us the building age, the location of the main stack and what the lease allows. Our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle those projects.

What to have ready when you call

  • A rough description of the project and where the fixtures will go.
  • The model numbers of any fixtures you have already bought.
  • Photos of the basement or utility space, including the water meter and main stack.
  • Any drawings, permits or notes from other trades.

If you are comparing quotes, ask what is included, such as permits, drywall access holes, hauling away old fixtures and a pressure test. Differences between quotes often come from what is left out.

The Ohio Plumbing Code, adopted by the Ohio Board of Building Standards and based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), sets the rules for venting, drain sizing and fixture connections, so your rough-in plan should be drawn to it.

Safety steps before work begins

  • Know where the main water shutoff is, and test that it turns.
  • Tell the plumber about any gas lines, electrical cables or old pipes you know of in the work area.
  • Clear the area and cover floors and furniture nearby.
  • If the work is in a basement, make sure there is light and a dry path to the panel and the shutoffs.

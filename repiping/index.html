Repiping in Westlake, OH

When supply pipes leak repeatedly, rust the water or choke pressure, repiping replaces them for good. Call (440) 468-0237 to talk through a plan. Dispatch answers 24/7.

Signs your supply lines are near the end

  • Brown or orange water when a tap has not been used for a while.
  • Low pressure at several fixtures that does not improve with new aerators.
  • A pattern of pinhole leaks in different spots, not one repair that failed.
  • Visible corrosion or flaking at joints in the basement.

Why galvanized and old copper cause trouble

Galvanized steel pipe corrodes from the inside. Rust builds up in the walls of the pipe and narrows it, which is why pressure drops. The rust can also flake and discolor water. Copper lasts much longer but can develop pinhole leaks from the inside, especially in water with certain chemistry.

Once a house has several failures, repeated spot repairs cost more in time and damage than a planned replacement. That is the point where repiping stops being a luxury.

Lead and the service line

Cleveland Water says some homes and buildings in its service area have lead service lines or plumbing that contains lead, and it maintains a service line inventory you can search by address. Its 2024 sampling found 1 of 52 samples above the 15 parts per billion action level, with a 90th-percentile result of 3.15 parts per billion.

Interior repiping replaces the pipes inside the house. The service line from the street to the meter is a separate matter, and Cleveland Water's inventory shows what material it has on record for your address. If it lists lead, ask the utility about next steps.

How a repipe is done

We map the existing runs, plan the new routing and open only the walls and ceilings we need. A house is usually without water for part of a day, and we plan stages so one bathroom and the kitchen can work as quickly as possible.

Drywall repair is a separate trade, so we tell you what to expect at each access point. For a single section rather than the whole house, ask about a partial repipe, and see leak detection if you are unsure where the leaks are.

Planning a bathroom remodel? A repipe goes well with new fixtures. See plumbing installation for how we coordinate the two.

What to ask any plumber before a repipe

Ask which material they recommend and why, how much of the wall and ceiling will be opened, how the water shutoff will be staged and what inspection the work needs. A plumber who answers these plainly is worth trusting with the job. You can also ask the Westlake Building Department what permit applies, and which licensing or registration your plumber works under.

Replacement piping in Ohio is installed under the Ohio Plumbing Code, which draws on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), so the materials and fittings used should be approved for potable water under that code.

What to do if a pipe fails before the repipe

  1. Shut off the main water valve at once.
  2. Open a low faucet to drain the pipes and ease pressure.
  3. Turn off the water heater power or gas if the tank may drain.
  4. Move belongings out of the wet area.
  5. Call for a plumber and describe where the water is coming from.

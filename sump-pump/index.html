Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Westlake, OH

A sump pump that stops during a storm can flood a basement in hours. Call (440) 468-0237, 24/7. Move belongings off the floor, check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker is on and keep a bucket handy.

A sump pump sits in a pit in the basement floor and pumps groundwater out of the house. In a wet spring, in snowmelt or during a heavy storm it may run constantly. When it fails, the water has nowhere to go but the floor.

We repair existing pumps, replace failed units and install new pits and battery backups. Because failures cluster around the same storms, calls come in waves, so earlier is better.

Backup power and a second pump

Storms often knock out power at the same time they flood basements, so a pump that depends on household electricity may stop when it is needed. A battery backup pump sits beside the primary and takes over if power fails or the primary can't keep up.

We size the backup to the pit and the likely inflow, and we explain how long a battery runs and how to test it. A water alarm on the pit floor is a cheap extra that gives you early warning.

Where the water goes matters

Sump discharge should go outdoors, away from the foundation, and not into a sanitary sewer line. Local rules differ, so ask the City of Westlake what applies to your house. Rocky River, for example, runs a sewer department that maintains both storm and sanitary lines, and it asks residents with water in a basement to call its service garage before a plumber. Check your own city's practice.

Installing a new pit

A new pit means cutting the basement slab, digging a basin, setting the pump and routing the discharge pipe. It is a one-day job in most houses. We discuss the best location, usually the lowest corner or where water first appears.

Related issues like damp walls or a floor that stays wet are covered under basement waterproofing. A failed lateral can also cause basement water, and a sewer camera inspection checks that.

Keep the pit clear of debris and make sure the discharge pipe is not frozen or blocked outside. A pump that runs but moves little water often has a clogged discharge or a failed check valve.

Where a sump discharge may go is a code and city question. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), sets rules for drainage, and your city adds its own, so ask Westlake before routing the pipe.

Safety steps when the basement is flooding

  1. Stay out of standing water if the furnace, outlets or panel are near it, because it may be live.
  2. Do not unplug a running pump from a wet floor.
  3. Shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  4. Move valuables up and off the floor.
  5. Call for help and tell dispatch how deep the water is.

Emergency Water Heater Repair in Westlake, OH

A water heater leaking steadily or giving no hot water needs attention today. Call (440) 468-0237, 24/7. If the tank is leaking, shut off the cold water supply to the heater and turn the power or gas control to off.

Most water heater calls fall into a handful of causes. Gas heaters lose their flame, electric heaters lose an element or thermostat, and any tank can leak at a fitting or through the tank itself. Many repairs can be finished in a single visit.

The first job is deciding whether the unit is worth repairing. A heater with a failed thermostat or element is a straightforward fix. A tank that is leaking from its shell is finished.

Hard water and your heater

Cleveland Water draws from Lake Erie, and its 2025 water quality report lists hardness (as calcium carbonate) between 105 and 130, averaging 117. Moderately hard water leaves mineral scale in water heaters over time. Scale insulates the bottom of a gas tank and coats electric elements until they burn out.

A yearly flush removes sediment before it hardens, and an anode rod check shows how the tank is protected from rust. Both are cheap compared with replacing a tank that failed early. We cover this in water heater replacement when a unit is past repair.

Repair or replace

Repair when

The tank is dry, the unit is not old, and the fault is a part such as an element, thermostat, thermocouple, valve or burner.

Replace when

The tank leaks from the shell, rust shows at the seams or repairs keep recurring. Water will not stop coming out of a failed tank.

Consider tankless when

You are replacing anyway and want continuous hot water. See tankless water heaters for the trade-offs.

Water heater leaking: emergency steps

Shut off the cold water to the heater, switch the electric breaker off or turn the gas control to pilot or off, and open a hot tap in the house to relieve pressure. Keep the floor around the unit clear.

A heater that is hot to the touch near the relief valve, or a relief valve that is dripping, should be looked at promptly. Do not plug the valve.

Safety steps we take on every visit

Any water heater visit includes a look at the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe, the venting on gas units and the condition of the connections. A gas unit also gets a check for gas smell and proper burner flame.

If we find something unsafe, such as a blocked vent or a corroded gas connector, we tell you plainly. It may be a small repair or a reason to retire the unit. Either way you decide with the facts in front of you.
